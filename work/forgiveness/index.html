Human Flourishing Program, Harvard · Strategy & platform · 2026

From a one-day workshop to a platform built to teach the next billion people how to forgive.

The Global Forgiveness Movement had a workbook tested with over 4,500 people in five high-conflict countries, and no home of its own. We ran the workshop, wrote the scope, and built the platform.

Starting point

Every piece worked. There was no single address to send someone to.

The REACH Forgiveness workbook, adapted by the Human Flourishing Program from Everett Worthington's research, is free, evidence-based, and effective. In 2026 it lived as PDFs on the program's site, forgiveness groups ran on Google Forms, and the videos sat on other organizations' channels. The team asked us for a website.

Before designing anything we went to Cambridge and spent a day with them on the questions underneath the website: what the movement is for, where they want it to be in ten years, and what a platform would have to do to help. We brainstormed everything it could be, then scoped it down to what could ship this year. Our first thinking in this territory was an earlier project, Sorry, a small web app from 2020 that teaches you how to give an apology that heals.

What we found

The videos aren't for the reader. They're for the person running the room.

The first draft of the scope assumed people would watch videos while working through the workbook alone. The clients' correction changed the shape of the whole site: the videos exist so that someone can lead a group. Participants do the lessons on their own, then meet, and a leader plays an eight-to-fifteen-minute video to open the discussion. So the homepage has exactly two doors: do it solo, or start a group.

Everything else follows from one measure the clients confirmed: help more people finish the workbook. Features that don't serve that sentence wait for a later version. And one promise stays literal on every page: what you write in the workbook never leaves your device.

Where we went

A platform the clients can run without us.

Five public pages in the workbook's own visual identity. Every edition and language of the workbook, free to download, no account required. A groups pathway that explains what a forgiveness group is, how to run one, and registers new groups so the program can count them. Free accounts with a member area for the leader video series, and an interactive twelve-lesson workbook that syncs your place, and only your place, when you sign in.

When we handed over the first version, we also built a second site to guide the review: where to look, what to look for, and how to write us a note. The feedback came back organized, row by row, and we could act on all of it.

What's next

Launch in November 2026.

The site is live behind a password while the team reviews it and the video series is filmed. At launch the clients own it outright, and the platform is built so they can keep it going with no dependence on us.
