ClaudeKit

An operating system for AI agents that run like a company, and teach the person running them.

AI coding agents are fast, capable, and forgetful. ClaudeKit gives them the structure of a small firm: a mentor, a crew, and two officers. We built this site with it, and until September 9 every push went through an officer's review.

Starting point

Inside the work, the gaps are hard to see.

While we built Pastry Pirates with AI agents, one session took on a thirty-five-item playtest. It fixed twenty-two items, checked four, and reported the work done. Nothing in its report was false. From inside the work, the gap was hard to see.

The second problem was memory. AI chat sessions may sound human, but they work in utterly inhuman ways. Each session starts blank, so decisions were made twice and all the judgment rested on the person directing the work.

What we found

The memory lasts. Each agent starts fresh.

The fixes were organizational, not technical. They mirror the way human organizations run. Accountability: someone other than the builder reviews the work, because fresh eyes see what the builder can't. Red-proofing: a failing check proves a defect exists before anyone fixes it. Institutional memory: each new agent first reads files that record what was decided and why, what the last reviewer found, and what waits on a human.

That freshness wears off. A reviewer who has been in the room all week comes to share your blind spots. Just like in the real world.

Where we went

A mentor, a crew, and two officers.

The mentor coaches the person, not the code. Before a request runs, it shows a clearer way to ask. The crew takes every task through the same steps. A measurer proves the fault. Builders work on separate files. A tester uses the real product. A checker reads the evidence, not just the report. A sweeper finds the same fault elsewhere.

The CTO looks after agents working unattended and keeps production safe. The CEO takes the person's request in their exact words and answers one question: did what they asked for happen? A one-page adapter in each repo gives the officers its facts, without copies that go stale.

What changed

It reviewed this site's headline before you read it.

The CEO's first review was the sentence at the top of our homepage. It found six bugs. Our takeaway: whenever we use a tool, we also build better ones, like a carpenter who spends an hour on a jig so each cut takes seconds. Fix the tool, redesign the system.
