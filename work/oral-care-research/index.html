A consumer oral-care brand · User research · 2026

Before the product launches, find out what people are really buying. It turns out to be a routine that holds.

A brand preparing a new product for retail asked us to find out how people actually look after their teeth at home, while there was still time to change the product.

Starting point

They knew the category and the shelf. They hadn't been in anyone's bathroom.

The brand had a product in development, a retail launch on the calendar, and a clear picture of the competition. What they didn't have was time with the people who would use it: what happens at 10pm when someone is tired, what a parent does with two children who like different things, why a person stops doing something they know is good for them.

They asked for user research ahead of the launch. We came on as an external research arm, so their team could keep building while we went and looked.

What we found

The product underneath the product is a routine that holds.

The most useful question we asked was the plainest one: walk us through your morning and your evening. Nobody described the object. They described the moment before it, when they decide whether to bother, and the moment after, when it either felt worth it or didn't.

That reframed the brief. The thing the brand is manufacturing is made of materials and packaging. The thing people are buying is a routine they can keep, and a routine survives or fails on a handful of small, specific conditions that have little to do with how the product performs on paper. When one of them goes missing, the habit goes with it, however good the product is.

What those conditions are belongs to the client. What we can say is that several of them were not what the brand expected, and that they changed what the product needs to be good at.

Where we went

A map the team could argue with.

We wrote the interview protocol, ran the interviews with product samples in hand, and read every transcript twice: once for what people said, once for what they did. Then, rather than deliver a document, we built the findings into an interactive map. Every takeaway sits on three axes: whether it's about the object or the world around it, whether it's about function or feeling, and how much evidence stands behind it.

That third axis is the one we care most about. It separates what the brand can brief a designer on today from what is still one person's voice, so the map is honest about its own limits. The whole thing is encrypted at rest and lives on a private address, because the research is theirs. The map, the passes over the transcripts, and the encryption around them were built with AI in days rather than weeks, so the budget went to talking to people rather than to building the thing that holds what they said.

What's next

Research continues through the launch.

The first round is in the team's hands and shaping the product. Further rounds follow as the design settles, so the last decisions before retail are made with people in the room rather than assumptions.
