Polycam · Strategy · 2026

Cited by AI

Testing whether Polycam could influence which videos AI cites, with eight rules and six videos.

Polycam's videos worked for viewers but were largely invisible to AI answer engines. The brief was to test whether Polycam could influence which videos those engines cite. The method: turn existing blog posts on topics people ask AI about into video scripts. We wrote rules first, then scripts and storyboards, checking sources throughout.

Starting point

Turn the blog posts into scripts, in Polycam's voice.

Polycam makes a 3D scanning app. This work was for architects, engineers, and contractors who use phone scans for renovation documents, material estimates, and scan-to-CAD workflows. Each blog article was to become about four hundred words of script, roughly three minutes on screen.

Polycam's draft pipeline had two gaps. Its drafts were written for viewers, so they opened with context and buried the answer an AI would lift out. And some claims had no third-party source.

What we found

A video speaks to more than its viewers.

Answer engine optimization, or AEO, is creating content such that an AI system answering a question quotes you. For a video, it reads the description, chapter markers, transcript, and, as far as we can tell, most reliably a dense closing summary. This structure also suited viewers: the answer came first, the hook second.

Where we went

Eight rules, and a fact basis stapled to every script.

The rules: answer the title's question in the first sentence. Make each section a question and answer that works when quoted alone. Source every number. Close on a dense summary written for extraction, not storytelling. Keep the call to action out of that summary. Be honest: disclose where Polycam stops working, because a stated limit reads as trustworthy. Match on-screen text to the spoken claim. Put the story of the problem after the answer.

Six videos were scripted, shot, edited, and launched using this pipeline. The first four covered a material takeoff from a phone scan, a scan-to-CAD DWG workflow, a comparison of five floor plan apps, and generative versus detection-based AI. Each script came with a fact-basis appendix: every claim about another company traced to its own pricing page or documentation, with the URL and date checked.

The comparison video was the hardest and needed its own research. The five companies' products make different outputs, use different pricing models, and need different devices. So we priced and specced one hypothetical test case for each product, at live prices, and held Polycam to the same standard. The script offered new, useful knowledge about the whole 3D scanning industry, and Polycam saw it cited by AI soon after launch.

What's next

The eight rules are now a system prompt.

Each new video starts from that prompt and a source article. Polycam reported the citation rate doubling in the month after the first videos went up. That is their measurement, not ours, and one month is short, so we treat it as an early signal, not a settled result. The engagement is ongoing.
