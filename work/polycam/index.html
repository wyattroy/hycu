Polycam · Strategy · 2026

Cited by AI

Video scripts written to be cited: an AEO framework for Polycam, and the six videos made with it.

Polycam's videos were working on people but were largely invisible to the AI systems those same people were asking. The brief was to produce a batch of videos to test the company's capacity to influence AI citations. We built the framework first, wrote new scripts and storyboards with it, and made source-checking a step in production rather than a check at the end.

Starting point

Turn the blog posts into scripts, in our voice.

Polycam makes a 3D scanning app, and the audience for this work is architects, engineers, and contractors using phone scans for renovation documents, material estimates, and scan-to-CAD workflows. The brief that arrived was to take existing blog articles, turn each into about four hundred words of script, roughly three minutes on screen. They had a draft pipeline and a shelf of articles to work from.

Two things about those drafts were not going to translate to the screen in a way LLM answer engines could use: they were written for a person watching, so they opened with context and buried the answer, which is the wrong shape for a machine trying to lift one out. And they carried claims that were unsubstantiated by third parties.

What we found

A video speaks to more than its viewers.

Answer engine optimization, or AEO, is the act of creating content such that an AI system answering someone's question quotes you rather than paraphrasing somebody else. For a blog post the mechanism is simple, because the model reads the text. For a video it reads the description, the chapter markers, the transcript, and, as far as we can tell, most reliably a dense summary paragraph at the close. This structure turned out not to fight with what keeps a person watching. If the first sentence states the answer, the hook can follow it rather than replace it.

Where we went

Eight rules, and a fact basis stapled to every script.

The framework we designed used eight structural rules. Open with one sentence that answers the question in the title. Build each section as a question and an answer that survives being quoted on its own. Attach a source to every number. Close on a dense summary paragraph written for extraction, rather than storytelling for a listener. Keep the call to action out of that paragraph so neither one dilutes the other. Be honest: disclose where Polycam stops working, because a stated limit reads as trustworthy rather than as a caveat. Match the on-screen text to the spoken claim. Put the pain narrative after the answer instead of in front of it.

Six videos have been scripted, shot, edited, and launched using this pipeline. The first four scripts covered a material takeoff from a phone scan, a scan-to-CAD DWG workflow, a comparison of five floor plan apps, and an explainer separating generative AI from the detection-based kind. Each script was delivered with a fact-basis appendix beside it: every competitor claim traced to that company's own pricing page or documentation, with the URL and the date we checked it.

The comparison video between competitor products was the hardest one that required its own research pipeline. Five companies' live pricing, on products that all generate different outputs, have different pricing models, and require different devices. In order to compare apples to oranges we decided to create one hypothetical test case that we would price and spec for each product, and hold Polycam to the same standard as the others. This allowed us to write a script that presented genuinely new and useful knowledge about the entire 3D scanning industry, and Polycam saw it cited by AI soon after launch.

What's next

The framework is the starting input now.

The eight rules are a system prompt, and it goes in alongside the source article to produce each next video. Polycam reported the citation rate doubling in the month after the first videos went up. That's their measurement rather than ours, and one month is a short window, so we'd treat it as an early signal instead of a settled result. The engagement is ongoing.
