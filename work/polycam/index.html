Polycam · Marketing strategy · 2026

Nine markets, four channels, one launch: a brand-awareness plan for every locality, built as a tool the team could use the same day.

Polycam's 3D capture app was about to work on Android phones and on iPhones without a LiDAR sensor. Their marketing lead asked for a spreadsheet of outlets to contact. We asked whether we could do better than a spreadsheet.

Starting point

Reach the people that architects, contractors, and insurers actually listen to.

We've worked with Polycam on marketing strategy since before Hycu had a name, so the brief arrived in shorthand. The launch, called device parity, opens the app to a much larger set of phones. The job was to build brand awareness in nine markets, from Germany to Japan, through four channels: micro-influencers, podcasts, press, and industry newsletters. The starting material was a workbook of a few hundred creators the team had already assessed.

The ask was a list. The need was for the team to send the right message to the right person, quickly, in nine countries, during launch week.

What we found

A market's language isn't a person's language.

Three things shaped the plan. First, the team's own workbook was the gold standard. Every decision they'd already made about a creator, including the ones they'd rejected, stayed visible rather than being filtered out by our reasoning. Second, no contact could be invented. Every row records where it came from and how confident we are, and an address we couldn't confirm is marked as such rather than guessed.

Third, the market column was choosing the language. An American creator with ten million subscribers was about to receive an email in Korean because his audience is in Seoul. Each contact now carries the language that person actually publishes in, which is not always the language of the market they reach.

Where we went

A page, not a report.

The deliverable is a single encrypted web page. It holds 507 contacts across nine markets and four channels, each with a drafted note in the right language, a line on why this person matters for this launch, and the team's own status for that contact. An earlier draft was both a report and a strategy document, and it was cut for it. The page exists so someone on the team can open it, find the German construction podcasts, and send.

The hardest part was the hooks: the one handwritten sentence in each note that ties Polycam to something that creator actually made. We audited every hook against the source it refers to, and a hook that isn't tied to a specific piece of work fails the build.

What's next

The launch is this month.

Device parity ships in September 2026 and the team sends from the page. We keep it current as replies come in, which is the part of a plan that usually gets lost the day after it's delivered.
