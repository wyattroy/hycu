How to Change Institutions

A palm-sized field guide to changing an institution, or any organization, from the inside, by working with it.

The book is for people inside schools, governments, hospitals, and large organizations who want change that lasts. It came out of a year of that work at one large university.

Starting point

There are two ways to change an institution: through a fight, or through care.

We spent a year inside a design school that had outgrown its building, helping it serve students better. Part of us wanted to rabble-rouse. The better part wanted to understand the person across the table, work through our own frustration first, and build a relationship that would outlast the issue.

We chose care, as an experiment. Would collaboration produce more change than conflict? Could we write the method down simply enough for someone else to use?

What we found

Assume the people in an institution care about each other, and invite them to act on it.

Every intervention that worked started as a conversation, became a collaboration, and ended as a friendship with someone on the faculty or staff. When we came with a demand, less changed. The institution was not a wall. It was a web of people. Most wanted what we wanted, and had constraints we couldn't see from outside.

The book bets that most institutions are like this, and that change comes from inviting people to be their best selves together.

Where we went

A spiritual self-help book, dressed as a business book.

It covers noticing what to change, working with an institution's complexity, building coalitions one relationship at a time, and keeping momentum after the first meeting. The key to all of it is letting go of your ego. Like Thich Nhat Hanh's small books, How to Walk and How to Love, it is short, direct, and meant to be carried. The acknowledgements fill a third of the pages, because the ideas belong to the people who inspired them. On this book, AI worked only as an editor. It interviewed us, read every draft, and pointed out gaps in the reasoning for us to fill. It wrote none of the book.

What changed

It became the studio's operating manual.

Every institutional client now gets a day in the room before any design, a scope they can carry out, and a plan that treats their constraints as material, not obstacles. The forgiveness platform and the Gund Hall research both use this method.
