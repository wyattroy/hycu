One dimension up.

Hycu is short for hypercube. It's the shape you get when you extend a cube in a direction that isn't there yet, and it's the way we look at a client's problem.

Pronounced: Hi-Q

Why a hypercube

Start with a point. Pull it sideways and you have a line. Pull the line and you have a square, pull the square and you have a cube. Every step adds a dimension, and every step lets you see the shape below it whole. A square is obvious once you can stand outside it.

Pulling a cube into a fourth dimension is harder to picture, and that difficulty is the point. To see a hypercube you have to reach into a direction you can't yet see and find a way to draw it anyway. That is what design is for, as we understand it: a process for making new and useful things, whether the thing is a product like a table, a system like a bus route, or an idea like space travel.

So on every project we try to step one level above the thing we were hired to design. Above the website is the way a program teaches. Above the product on the shelf is the routine it lives in. Above the spreadsheet is the launch. From up there the shape of the problem is clear, and the useful thing to build is usually obvious.

How we work

Every engagement starts with the same two questions: where are you now, and where do you want to go? We answer the first with research, because most organizations know less about their own situation than they think. We answer the second with you, then propose more than one route between the two, at more than one budget, so the choice is yours and the plan is one you can actually carry out.

Then, more often than not, we build it. A brief becomes a tool. A scope becomes a platform. When we handed a client the first version of their site, we also built a second site to guide their feedback, so they knew where to look and how to write us notes. Removing friction from the work is part of the work.

We take two shapes of engagement. A narrowly scoped project, a month or two, with a defined deliverable. Or an external arm of your team on a longer arrangement, which scales up and back as you need it. Most of our clients start with the first and continue with the second.

What we don't do

We're curious about the systems behind objects, spaces, people, and culture. Work that stays inside a single medium is better done by a specialist in that medium.

  • Video productionWe'll help you decide what a film is for. We won't make the film.
  • Virtual realitySame.
  • ArchitectureWe'll study how a building is used. We aren't architects.
  • StylingMaking something prettier without changing what it does.
